Discharge from the hospital typically begins with a conversation between the patient (and family, if involved) and the care team about readiness to leave, expected recovery timeline, and any needed follow-up care. Patients receive written discharge instructions covering medications, activity restrictions, warning signs that should prompt a return to care, and scheduled follow-up appointments. It's common for discharge to take a few hours after the decision is made, due to paperwork, prescription processing, and final checks by nursing and physician staff.

Patients are encouraged to arrange transportation home in advance, since many hospitals require a responsible adult to accompany patients discharged after sedation or certain procedures. Prescriptions can often be filled directly through the hospital's outpatient pharmacy before leaving, which can help avoid delays. If a patient needs additional support after discharge — such as home health care, medical equipment, or a rehabilitation facility placement — a case manager or social worker typically coordinates this before the patient leaves. Questions after discharge can usually be directed to the discharging unit or a 24-hour nurse advice line.
