Emergency departments use a triage process to prioritize patients by the severity of their condition rather than arrival time, so wait times can vary significantly and are not first-come-first-served. Upon arrival, a triage nurse will typically ask about symptoms, take vital signs, and assign a priority level; patients with life-threatening conditions (such as chest pain, difficulty breathing, or severe trauma) are seen immediately.

Patients are usually registered either before or shortly after being seen by a provider, and will be asked for identification and insurance information when possible — care is never delayed for registration in a true emergency. Family members are generally allowed to accompany patients in the ER, though space and infection-control policies may limit the number of visitors, especially in trauma or resuscitation bays. If your condition is not life-threatening, ask the triage nurse or front desk about expected wait times, and let staff know if your symptoms worsen while waiting. Hospitals with a trauma center designation (Level I–IV) have additional specialized staff and equipment for the most severe emergency and trauma cases.
