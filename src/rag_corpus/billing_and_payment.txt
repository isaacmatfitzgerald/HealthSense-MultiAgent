Hospital bills are typically issued after insurance claims have been processed, which can take several weeks depending on the payer. An itemized statement is available on request from the billing department at any time, even before the final bill is issued. Most hospitals accept payment by credit card, debit card, check, or money order, and many offer an online patient portal for viewing and paying bills electronically.

Financial assistance and charity care programs are generally available for patients who qualify based on income and household size — ask to speak with a financial counselor, usually available during business hours, either in person or by phone. Interest-free payment plans can typically be arranged directly with the billing office for balances that can't be paid in full. If you believe a charge is incorrect, you can request a billing review; most hospitals ask that disputes be raised within 30–60 days of the statement date. Uninsured or self-pay patients are often eligible for a prompt-pay discount if the balance is paid in full by the due date.
