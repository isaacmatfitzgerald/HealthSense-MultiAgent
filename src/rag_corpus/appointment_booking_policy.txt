Appointments can typically be scheduled by phone through the hospital's central scheduling line, through an online patient portal, or via a referral from another physician. New patients are usually asked to provide their insurance information, a photo ID, and the reason for the visit when scheduling; a referral or prior authorization may be required for certain specialists depending on your insurance plan. Same-day or urgent appointments are sometimes available for time-sensitive concerns — ask when scheduling whether an urgent slot is available if your regular provider is booked out.

When arriving for an appointment, patients should bring a photo ID, their insurance card, a list of current medications, and any relevant prior test results or referral paperwork. Arriving 15–30 minutes early is generally recommended for new-patient paperwork or check-in.

Cancellations are usually requested at least 24 hours in advance; many hospitals apply a no-show or late-cancellation fee for missed appointments without sufficient notice, particularly for specialist or procedure visits. Rescheduling is typically free of charge when done with adequate notice.

Telemedicine (video or phone) visits are commonly available for follow-up care, medication management, and some initial consultations where an in-person exam isn't required. Patients will usually need a device with a camera and a stable internet connection, and should confirm with their provider's office whether their specific visit type is eligible for telemedicine before scheduling.
